Racomitrium Brid. Three species of Racomitrium have been reported from Lord Howe Island, but none are known to occur there. ‘Racomitrium aciculum’ was reported for the island by Ramsay (1984), but there is no such species. The source of the report is unknown (H. Ramsay, pers. comm. November 2016), and the report is therefore discounted. The identity of Rhacomitrium leptostomoides J.B.Först., the type of which Förster (1901) attributed to Lord Howe Island, has been the subject of some confusion. Brotherus (1901–1909) considered it to be a synonym of R. lanuginosum (Hedw.) Brid., but Vitt et al. (1993) considered it to be a synonym of R. japonicum (Vitt et al. 1993), which was consequently attributed to Lord Howe Island (Streimann and Klazenga 2002). However, Vitt et al. (1993) said it was uncertain whether the original material of R. leptostomoides was in fact collected on Lord Howe Island. Considering that R. japonicum (and R. lanuginosum) are cold-climate species, and that no other specimens of Racomitrium are known from Lord Howe Island, this is a reasonable assumption. The type of Racomitrium leptostomoides is J.B. Moore 30, attributed to Lord Howe Island on a label accompanying the holotype (Vitt et al. 1993). J.B. Moore was a collector in Tasmania in the 1890s and is not known to have collected outside that state. I think that a simple error occurred, as follows: J.B. Moore sent specimens to Ferdinand von Mueller in Melbourne, who regularly sent material to bryologists in Europe for identification, particularly Enrst Hampe and Viktor Brotherus. Von Mueller had also received specimens from Lord Howe Island collected by Charles Moore, which were also sent to Europe. It would have been an easy error to make to include J.B. Moore’s Tasmanian collection with Charles Moore’s collections, with all of them attributed to Lord Howe Island. 47 A collection by J.B. Moore with the same field number is in MEL (MEL-1037580A), dated September 1893. Although it is Trichocolea mollissima, it is from Tasmania and seems likely to be a segregate of the original gathering, since there are no duplicated numbers among J.B. Moore’s collections (AVH 2018). I believe it is very likely that ‘J.B. Moore’ (about whom nothing is known) is actually an incorrect name, and that all collections ascribed to this person were actually collections by T.B. (Thomas Bather Moore), a prospector and explorer about whom much is known (McShane 1983). In the handwriting of the time a flourished T would be easily misinterpreted as a J, and it seems unlikely that two people with the same middle and last names were collecting plants in western Tasmania at the same time and both sending them to von Mueller in Melbourne. Ptychomitriaceae Schimp. The family Ptychomitriaceae includes seven genera: Aulacomitrium, Brachypodium, Campylostylium, Henoniella, Notarisia, Ptychomitriopsis, Ptychomitrium. Only Ptychomitrium occurs on Lord Howe Island. This genus was fully revised for Australia as part of this study (Meagher 2017). The following treatment follows that revision. Ptychomitrium Fürnr. The genus Ptychomitrium consists of about 21 species, of which five have been reported from Australasia: (Scott and Stone 1976, Fife 1995, Cao et al. 2001, Cao et al. 2005, Thouvenot and Bardat 2010). Hampe (1874) reported Ptychomitrium muelleri (Mitt.) A.Jaeger from Lord Howe Island. Over 100 years later, Ramsay (1984) added P. australe (Hampe) A.Jaeger to the island’s flora, and recently Cao et al. (2005) added P. acutifolium Hook.f. & Wilson. Ptychomitrium laxifolium (Mull.Hal.) Paris was originally described as an Australian endemic but had not been critically reviewed before this study. The Australian species of Ptychomitrium have never been treated in their entirety. Instead, species have been gradually added, subtracted and synonymised, resulting in considerable confusion about their identities. The confusion has been worsened by a lack of detailed illustrations of the species, and by a consequential plethora of incorrectly identified specimens in Australian and New Zealand herbaria. Fürnrohr (1829: 19) erected the genus Ptychomitrium with P. polyphyllum as the type species. --- Australia by Meagher (2003) based on three collections from tropical Queensland. Trichocoleaceae Nakai The family Trichocoleaceae is characterised by 2–4 pinnate branching and multiply divided leaves and underleaves in which the terminal lobes are ciliate, so that the plants have a distinctive woolly or spiny appearance (Engel and Glenny 2008). Four genera are recognised: Castanoclobos Glenny and J.J.Engel, Eotrichocolea R.M.Schust., Leiomitra Lindb., and Trichocolea Dumort. (Engel and Glenny 2008, Crandall-Stotler et al. 2009). Plants on Lord Howe Island fit within Trichocolea most obviously because of their tripinnate branching (pinnate or sporadically bipinnate in other genera). Trichocolea Dumort. The genus Trichocolea includes about 35 currently accepted taxa (Tropicos 2016), of which 17 have not been considered in the literature since they were first described. The genus has not been monographed in its entirety, although Hatcher (1958a) monographed it for North, Central and South America. Hatcher (1958b) and Engel and Glenny (2008) treated the New Zealand species in detail, but there has been no formal treatment for species in the Australasian region as a whole. As a result there is considerable confusion about the correct names that should be applied to species in the region. Five species are currently reported from Australia: Trichocolea mollissima (Hook.f. & Taylor) Gottsche, T. rigida R.M.Schust., T. pluma (Reinw., Blume and Nees) Mont, T. minutifolia Steph. and T. wattsiana Steph. Common throughout our area in a variety of forest types from sea level to ca. 950 m, especially those that are rich and particularly wet. It occurs on the forest floor often in damp leaf litter (then erect), in often thick masses on logs that are totally bryophyte covered, on old stumps and tree bases (then horizontal to ± pendulous), on tree-fern trunks, and on bryophyte- covered banks (at times along stream courses). WIKIPEDIA (1 entry)

Trichocolea mollissima is a large liverwort of the Trichocoleaceae family. It is found commonly throughout wet sclerophyll and rainforests in Australia and New Zealand. In Australia it is present in Tasmania, Victoria, New South Wales and Queensland. It also occurs extensively on the north island of New Zealand. Its most distinctive feature is its highly divided, densely arranged and extremely small leaves, which lend it a woolly appearance; hence its common name “woolly worm”. It usually grows in a matted or tufted habit on the ground and lower trunks of trees, and exhibits a preference for decaying wood and deep shade. On casual observance, its leaf arrangement and growth habit resemble that of a large moss. Its stems are commonly 3–6 cm long, with leaves up to 0.6mm wide, with up to 3-3 divisions. leaves and stems are arranged in bi-pinnate form. It is commonly coloured pale-yellow-green, which fades to white or blue-green upon desiccation in drier conditions.
