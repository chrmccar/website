I examined the isotype of Riccardia wattsiana (NSW-251628!) and also a high-resolution
digital image of the holotype (G-11057!d) and two isotypes (G-12209!d, G-69441!d) of

Queensland, I believe that Brown (1989) was correct, and Scott’s ‘R. wattsiana’ is indeed
R. nitida.
Furuki (1991) cited G-12209 as the holotype of Riccardia graeffei, but annotated both that
specimen and G-69441 as ‘holotype’ on the packets, probably because both are from the same
gathering (Graeffe 1629). G-12209 should be considered the holotype and G-69441 an isotype.

The first known collections of Frullania from Lord Howe Island were made by William Watts
in 1911 (Stephani and Watts (1914), but the Frullania flora of the island was largely ignored
until 1981, when Dale Vitt collected bryophytes on Lord Howe Island, including some mosses
that were then undescribed (Vitt and Ramsay 1985, Vitt 1991). Among his other collections
were specimens of Frullania. Based largely on this collection, Sinske Hattori published a paper
on the Frullania flora of the island (Hattori 1987). He listed for the island three species already
known — Frullania ericoides (Nees) Mont., Frullania falciloba (Hook. & Taylor) Lehm. and
Frullania howeana Steph.) — and three new species — Frullania fulfordiae S.Hatt., Frullania
subincumbens S.Hatt. and Frullania vittiana S.Hatt. — and rejected several species previously
reported.
During 2009 I collected numerous specimens of Frullania from a wide range of habitats on the
island. Among these were four species that Hattori did not list: F. baladina Gottsche ex Steph.,
F. pentapleura Taylor, F. pycnantha (Hook.f. & Taylor) Taylor, F. rubella Gottsche ex Steph.
and F. junghuhniana Colenso. I did not find Frullania subincumbens (see discussion under that
species). I also did not collect F. falciloba (see discussion under F. pycnantha). A subsequent
study of specimens from the island held in CANB and NSW did not add further species to this
list.
The synopsis presented here is based only on plants from Lord Howe Island, and might not
represent the entire variation in those species that have a broader geographical range. The
following key may be used to identify species.

Frullania pycnantha (Hook.f. & Taylor) Taylor ex Gottsche, Lindenberg & Nees
Jungermannia pycnantha Hook.f. & Taylor, London J. Bot. 3: 566 (1844); Frullania pycnantha (Taylor)
Taylor ex Gottsche, Lindenberg & Nees, Syn. Hepat. 411 (1845)
Type: NEW ZEALAND, without locality, ?Colenso s.n., ?E-2570!d, ?E-2571!d, ?E-2572!d,
?E-2573!d.
Description: Hattori (1983).
Illustration: Hattori (1983, Figure 64).
Habitat: Epiphytic and sometimes lithophytic in dry to wet forest.
Distribution on LHI: Throughout the island.
Total distribution: Australia (NSW, Qld, LHI, Norfolk I.), New Zealand.
Discussion: This species is outwardly similar to F. rubella (Qld, NSW, LHI), F. engelii (Tas)
and F. setchellii (NZ) in having a basically 3-keeled perianth covered in tuberculae, rounded
bilobed underleaves without additional armature, and claw-like leaf lobules with a variably
truncate apex. It differs from F. rubella Gottsche ex Steph. in having larger lobules in
comparison to the lobes, larger and more orbicular underleaves, and more laciniate gynoecial
bracts; from F. engelii S.Hatt. by having leaves not squarrose (except when moist) and the
gynoecial bract roundly lobed at the apex; and from F. setchellii Pearson in having the
underleaves much more deeply lobed and the gynoecial bracts and bracteoles more laciniate.
Reports of Frullania falciloba from Lord Howe Island based on sterile specimens (Hattori
1987) were errors for this species. F. falciloba has a smooth perianth that is T-shaped in cross-
section. In the absence of perianths it can be distinguised from F. pycnantha by its leaves not
being squarrose and by the much narrower and shallower notch in the underleaf apex.
Hooker and Taylor (1844) did not identify the collector of the type material, so that although it
seems very likely that the specimens cited above (all filed as isosyntypes) are types, it is
impossible to be certain.
