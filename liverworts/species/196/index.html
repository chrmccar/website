BAZZANIA_AUSTRALIA_2019 (1 entry)

Plants mid- to dark green or brownish-green, forming loose mats on tree trunks. Shoots up to 7 mm wide, stems ~180–200 μm in diameter. Leaves triangular-ovate, mostly 2.5–3.5 mm long and 1.2–1.5 mm wide, apex distinctly 3-fid, the lobes shortly triangular, rather blunt. Underleaves large, quadrate to rectangular, narrowing slightly towards the apex, typically 0.8–1.0 mm wide × 1.2–1.4 mm long, margins entire to weakly crenate; narrowly connate with a leaf on one side only; 1 or 2 marginal rows of hyaline cells in the apical half. Distribution: Australia (Tasmania), New Zealand.

The most striking feature of this species is the unique form and texture of the underleaves, which are ± regularly divided into 4-6 slenderly acuminate, rigid lobes (Pig. 99: 7) and are composed entirely of thick-walled cells, with well-developed trigones (Pig. 99: 8). The other nonvittate, opposite-leaved Bazzania species in New Zealand have undivided underleaves, with a variously developed hyaline sector composed of leptodermous cells. Bazzania nidita and B. tayloriana have regularly lobed underleaves that are composed of decol- orate cells, entirely or in major part, and have alternate, vittate leaves. The underleaf lobes of Bazzania nova often appear twisted, with the spur-like, lateral projections projecting abaxially, reminiscent of the projecting spines on the underleaf lobes of Lepidozia setigera. Most likely to be confused with Bazzania hochstetteri because the leaves of both species are caducous and both are usually epiphytic. The underleaves of B. nova differ from those of B. hochstetteri in lacking any hyaline cells and in having conspicuously long marginal lobes. Sect. Vittatae (Steph.) Pulford Mastigobryum sect. The most striking feature of this species is the unique form and texture of the underleaves, which are ± regularly divided into 4-6 slenderly acuminate, rigid lobes (Pig. 99: 7) and are composed entirely of thick-walled cells, with well-developed trigones (Pig. 99: 8). The other nonvittate, opposite-leaved Bazzania species in New Zealand have undivided underleaves, with a variously developed hyaline sector composed of leptodermous cells. Bazzania nidita and B. tayloriana have regularly lobed underleaves that are composed of decol- orate cells, entirely or in major part, and have alternate, vittate leaves. The underleaf lobes of Bazzania nova often appear twisted, with the spur-like, lateral projections projecting abaxially, reminiscent of the projecting spines on the underleaf lobes of Lepidozia setigera. Most likely to be confused with Bazzania hochstetteri because the leaves of both species are caducous and both are usually epiphytic.
