      Differentiation: Radula tasmanica can be distinguished from other Radula species
by the combined occurrence of a number of characters I) When fresh , its mid green
colouration, the plants usually lack brown, orange and yellow tints, although plants from
exposed situations may be difficult to separate from R. uvifera by colour alone. 2) The de-
scending growth habit, which in vigourous plants tends to aggregate the stems into a mat.
3) The complete absence of amentulose and microphyllous branching. 4) The perianths
without plicae. 5) The obtuse lobe apex. 6) The interior lobule margin attached to the stem
for its entire length . 7) The 2- 5 small, ellipsoid oil bodies in each lobe cell, that contain a
spherical hyaline internal body, or 'eye-drop'. The oil-bodies of R. tasmanica are highly
distinctive, and are useful for identification, as R. plicata and R. sainsburiana are the only
other New Zealand species with similar oil-bodies. Radula tasmanica is readily distin-
guished from R. plicata by I) the lobe cell walls having (usually) distinct concave trigones
at their angles, where the walls of R. plicata lack any thickening, 2) the lobes not falcate,
compared to the strongly falcate lobes of R. plicata , 3) the lobules being rectangular, rather
than rhomboidal in outline, 4) the exterior portion of the lobe inrolled along a line extrapo-
lated from the keel, giving the lobe the appearence of a baseball glove, whereas the lobe in-
rolled along a line parallel to the stem, or not at all in R. plicata, 5) the absence of plicae on
the perianth, as opposed to the conspicuous plicae of R. plicata. From R. sainsburiana, R.
tasmaniccl can be distinguished by its I) larger size, 2) dioicous condition, and 3) rectangu-
lar lobules.
      In some situations, R. tasmanica bears a striking resemblence to R. uVljera, especially
where the two grow together in high light situations. However, R. lasmanica is easily dis-
tinguished by I) the complete absence of amentulose branching, as opposed to amentulose
branches usually produced every second leaf pair, although they may also be infrequently
produced and widely spaced in some plants of R. uvi{era, 2) the oil bodies which are small,
2- 5 per cell, opposed to the single, large coarsely botryoidal oil body of R. uvifera cells 3)
the absence of lamellae on the perianth, whereas R. uvifera bears 3- 14 rows of lamellae on
each surface of the perianth.
      Radula tasmanica can be distinguished from R. physoloba by I) the lack of any yel-
lowish colouration, opposed to the strong golden yellow green of R. physoloba, 2) the con-
sistently descending growth habit of R. tasmanica which virtually never grows upwards on
vertical substrates as R. physoloba does, 3) the lobule apex being obtuse rather than the
ac ute to acuminate as in R. physoloba 4) the complete absence ofmicrophyllous branching,
and 5) the 2- 5 small rather than one large oil body per lobe cell.
      Radula tasmanica can be distinguished from Radu/a tabu/aris by the lobule morphol-
ogy which in the latter has the carinal region narrowly inflated, and closely adpressed to the
lobe . When detached and flattened however, the lobules of both species appear very simi-
lar. Castle utilised perianth morphology to distinguish the two species, which in R. tasman-
ica is "e longate, truncate-clavate, terete below and gradually increasing in diameter" and in

     NOTES: Radula tasmanica has 'traditionally' been confused with R. physoloba. In
her paper on Radula, Hodgson (1944) quotes directly from Castle (1937) regarding how to
avoid confusing these two species " ... the cladogynous habit as well as other important di-
agnostic characters [of R. physoloba] have been overlooked with the result that the concep-
tion of the species has been extended in general to include two other forms which occur
within the same limits of distribution . One of these is R. tasmanica, which was described in
the Species Hepaticarum by Stephani, in 1909. This species is always smaller and the cari-
nal portion of the leaves is only narrowly inflated. It is, moreover, acrogynous and must be

take heed this advice, and of the specimens of R. tasmanica we have seen identified by her,
everyone is determined as R. physoloba.

     Radula tasmanica has very inflated leaf lobules and some plants resemble R. physolo-
ba. This species has in the past been confused with R. tabularis, an African species which
is quite different, not only in the shape of the leaves but also in the branches. Reports of R.
caespitosa by Hodgson (1965) are all referable to R. tasmanica. Weymouth collected ex-
tensively in various parts of Tasmania from 1899- 1914 and collections from Williamsford,
the type locality of R. tasmanica, all belong to this species. Many of these collections by
Weymouth were identified by Stephani (as written on the specimen labels) as R. physoloba.
Some of these were cited by Castle (1963: 38) as R. tasmanica, but some as R. tabularis
(Castle 1963: 41), and three collections, Levier n. 5523, n. 3659 and Weymouth 1453 were
cited under both species.
      Radula ratkowskiana resembles R. tasmanica in all its features, including the perianth
with a narrow base (Fig. 6f). According to the original description, R. ratkowskiana is char-
acterized by having a dorsal papilla over each lumen. However, this "papilla over each
lumen" is also found in the type material of R. tasmanica and even in some collections of
R. physoloba from Lord Auckland's Group, legit Hooker (Fig. 4e, insert). The occurrence
of these papillae is rather sporadic and those in the type material of R. ratkowskiana as well
as some collections from the type locality of R. tasmanica are rather well developed (Fig.
7a, b).
