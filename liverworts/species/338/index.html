The family Metzgeriaceae includes about 250 named species in four genera: Apometzgeria, Austrometzgeria, Metzgeria and Vandiemenia (Tropicos 2016). Only Metzgeria is known from Lord Howe Island. Metzgeria Raddi. So (2002a) noted that Metzgeria includes about 240 named species (So 2002a), of which 58 had been recorded from Australasia and some island groups of the Pacific Ocean. Her treatment reduced those 58 to 18 species, of which seven species have been reported from Australia, nine from New Zealand (Glenny 1998) and three from New Caledonia (So 2002a). However, various authors (e.g. Gradstein and Costa 2003, Costa 2008, Thouvenot et al. 2011) have disagreed with some aspects of So’s treatment; for example, Thouvenot et al. (2011) recognised seven species from New Caledonia. Until a complete world-wide treatment of the genus is undertaken, the taxonomy of Metzgeria will continue to be unsettled. Metzgeria leptoneura and M. decipiens (as M. furcata) have been reported previously from Lord Howe Island (Stephani and Watts 1914, So 2002a). M. lindbergii is new to the island’s flora. Discussion: Grolle (2002) and So (2002a) were I think incorrect in treating Metzgeria decipiens as a synonym of the circumglobal M. furcata. The number of cells covering the midrib on the ventral side distinguishes the two clearly: 2 (rarely 3) in M. decipiens, and 4 (rarely 3) in 179 M. furcata. A further difference is that M. furcata rarely produces gemmae on the margins of unmodified thalli (Atherton et al. 2010), whereas M. decipiens commonly produces such gemmae (Scott 1985). Until a complete assessment of the two taxa can be made, the two should be maintained as separate species. As a result of this confusion the overall distribution of M. decipiens in Australia is unclear. However, from my own observations it is common throughout the eastern seaboard from southern Tasmania to northern New South Wales, but reaching as far north as the Queensland Wet Tropics, where it is uncommon. Metzgeria caledonica Steph. was synonymised under M. decipiens by Kuwahara (1966), but Thouvenot et al. (2011) treated it as a synonym of M. ciliata Raddi and considered all reports of M. decipiens from New Caledonia to be M. ciliata. However, I am not convinced without a detailed study that the neotropical M. ciliata is likely to occur in the western Pacific, and take the conservative view that M. decipiens is the name that should be applied to collections from this region. Metzgeria leptoneura is easily distinguished from M. decipiens by the number of cells overlying the midrib (2–3 dorsally and 2–4 ventrally) and the irregularly thickened cell walls. Scott (1985) incorrectly described the hairs as single. Metzgeria australis Steph. was described by Stephani (1889) from several collections, including one from Lord Howe Island (locality unknown) ascribed to Chevalier de la Camara in 1882, although probably collected by someone else (Meagher 2014). Stephani (1899) subsequently synonymised it under M. nitida Mitt., a synonym of M. leptoneura, and Stephani and Watts (1914) thus reported M. nitida from Lord Howe Island. So (2002a) studied all the syntypes of M. australis and concluded that M. australis should be maintained as a synonym of M. leptoneura.
