It is confined to wet forest and rainforest, especially ) D Nothofagus rainforest, usually creeping over or among other bryophytes on the trunks of trees. The shoots are minute, usually less than 1 mm wide, with a rather grey-green colour caused by a combination of a papillose surface and brown oil bodies in the cells. The succubous leaves are unequally bilobed, like Tylimanthus tenellus. The smaller lobe can be quite prominent or almost absent. Although locally common in suitable habitats, in overall terms this species is rare in the region. The shoots of Z. argentea form flat green mats in wet forest, rainforest and fern gullies. The leaves are reduced to mere stubs, each of two large cells, giving them a slightly lobed appearance; they are arranged alternately along the stem and are inserted longitudinally.
