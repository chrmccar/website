             Studies on Lophocoleaceae. XIX. The systematic identity of
             Cyanolophocolea R.M. Schust., an intriguing liverwort from
              New Zealand and Australia, based on morphological and
                                molecular evidence

                                                  John J. Engel1,3 and Xiaolan He2

               1
                 Department of Botany, Field Museum, Chicago, IL 60605-2496, U.S.A.; 2 Botanical
                Museum, Finnish Museum of Natural History, University of Helsinki, P.O. Box 7,
                                         FIN-00014, Helsinki, Finland

               ABSTRACT. Cyanolophocolea echinella R.M.Schust. is an intriguing species, possessing some
               character states characteristic of the genus Chiloscyphus, such as the ability to produce both male
               and female gametangia on leading shoots, while having other features that are generic characters of
               Heteroscyphus, namely presence of gametangia on short, abbreviated lateral-intercalary branches.
               Its systematic identity was here studied using morphological and DNA sequence data of the
               chloroplast gene regions of rbcL, rps4 and trnL-F. Thirty-seven exemplars including eight of
               Cyanolophocolea echinella, 27 of Lophocoleaceae and two of Plagiochilaceae were used for
               phylogenetic reconstruction using both maximum parsimony and Bayesian inference methods.
               Our analyses placed Cyanolophocolea echinella within Heteroscyphus with strong support.
               Heteroscyphus echinellus (Lindenb. & Gottsche) J.J.Engel & XiaoL.He is proposed and based on
               morphological variations two varieties, var. echinellus and var. hyalinus J.J.Engel, are recognized. A
               large indel (59 base pairs) and one substitution in the trnL intron region, and also one substitution
               in the intergenic spacer region were found in the trnL-F in two Cyanolophocolea echinella
               exemplars both with hyaline oil-bodies. This indicates rapid molecular evolution might have
               occurred within the species.

               KEYWORDS. Chiloscyphus, Cyanolophocolea, Heteroscyphus, Marchantiophyta, rbcL, rps4, trnL-F,
               phylogeny, taxonomy.

Heteroscyphus allodontus (Hook.f. & Taylor) J.J.Engel & R.M.Schust. was reported from Lord
Howe Island by Stephani and Watts (1914) on the basis of a collection by Watts (Watts LHI
76b, NSW-818150). Unfortunately this collection could not be found during this study.
However, it is notable that there are no collections by Watts of Heteroscyphus argutus from the
island, despite it being very common in Erskine Valley where Watts 76b was collected. Poorly
developed specimens of H. argutus are quite common among collections from Lord Howe
Island, often bearing only two or three teeth at the apex and with the underleaf armature
reduced. It is possible that Watts 76b is merely such a weak form of that species, equivalent to
Schiffner’s variety paucidentatus.
It is noteworthy that the type, from the Auckland Islands, was growing on the bark of trees
(Taylor and Hooker 1845), which is a very unusual habitat for Geocalycaceae generally, and
that the species has never been fully described or illustrated, so that the identification of the
numerous specimens in New Zealand herbaria (AVH 2018) must be considered tentative.
At present I prefer to consider Heteroscyphus allodontus as ‘uncertain’ for Lord Howe Island,
pending an assessment of Watts 76b when it is found.

Lophocolea echinella Lindenb. & Gottsche, in Gottsche, Lindenberg & Nees, Syn. Hepat. 5: 703 (1847);
Chiloscyphus echinellus (Lindenb. & Gottsche) Mitt., in Hooker, Fl. Nov.-Zel. 2(2): 141 (1854);
Cyanolophocolea echinella (Lindenb. & Gottsche) R.M.Schust., Nova Hedwigia 72: 102 (2001);
Heteroscyphus echinellus (Lindenb. & Gottsche) J.J.Engel & X.L.He, Bryologist 113: 115 (2010)
Description: This species was comprehensively described by Engel and He (2010).
Illustrations: Scott (1985, Figure 84, 85), Engel and He (2010, Figures 3–5).
Habitat: Soil, bark and rotting wood in damp to wet situations.
Distribution on LHI: Summit Plateau of Mt Gower, on rotting wood in very wet situations in
deep gullies.
Total distribution: Eastern Australia (Tasmania to southern Queensland, Lord Howe I.), New
Zealand.
Discussion: Heteroscyphus echinellus was previously known only from New Zealand,
Tasmania and Victoria (Engel and He 2010). Lord Howe Island is thus the mostly northerly
known point of its distribution. The variety echinellus was thought to have been endemic to
New Zealand, with only H. echinellus var. hyalinus J.J.Engel known from Australia (Engel and
He 2010). This variety is therefore new to the Australian flora. It is easily distinguished from
var. hyalinus even in the field because it has plane rather than downcurved leaf apices, so that
the spinose armature around the apex is clearly visible in dorsal view (Engel and He 2010).
