(Scale bars: A,B = 2 mm; C,D,G = 0.5 mm; E,F = 100 µm.) 218 Discussion: Chiloscyphus howeanus belongs in subgenus Lophocolea section Lophocolea on account of (1) a smooth leaf surface, (2) chiloscyphoid aspect, (3) perianth lacking longitudinal lamellae, (4) leaves bilobed, and (5) rare ventral-intercalary branching. This section in general includes species with shallowly to deeply bilobed leaves and deeply divided underleaves. Engel (2010) recognised 10 species of section Lophocolea from Australasia. The common but not constant occurrence of a leaf-free strip on the dorsal side of stems in C. howeanus is somewhat problematic because it is taxonomically important (Engel 2010). A leaf-free strip is otherwise known only in two other species in section Lophocolea: C. anisolobus J.J.Engel & Glenny and C. excisifolius (Steph.) J.J.Engel & R.M.Schust. C. anisolobus is immediately distinct from C. howeanus in having more or less oblong leaves with the leaf very commonly 3-fid, and a cyathiform perianth. C excisifolius differs in having leptodermous leaf cells lacking trigones, smaller underleaves (to 1.4× the stem width) with more laciniate lobes ending in a uniseriate row of up to 9 cells, and the sac of each androecial bract fused with the sac on the opposite bract; furthermore, gynoecia are unknown in C. excisifolius. To a very large extent Chiloscyphus howeanus also shares features with C. lentus (Hook.f. & Taylor) J.J.Engel & R.M.Schust. and C. subporosus (Mitt.) J.J.Engel & R.M.Schust. particularly in the general form of the leaves, underleaves, gynoecial bracts and perianth. Neither of those species has a leaf-free strip on the stem, but as this is intermittent in C. howeanus its significance is uncertain. Chiloscyphus lentus differs otherwise in having leaves symmetric to subsymmetric, never with a long-decurrent dorsal margin. Its perianth is also dentate-laciniate rather than broadly spinose, and the saccate part of each androecial bract is fused with the opposite bract (Engel 2010). Chiloscyphus subporosus differs most obviously in having the leaves overlapping the stem midline dorsally and with the insertion strongly succubous, and in the segments of the perianth lobes being commonly armed with several opposing spinose teeth. Other differences in that species are the underleaves often being armed with extra teeth, saccate portions of androecia fused to the bract opposite and armed with numerous teeth, and underleaves generally with a much higher disc at the central sinus (commonly up to 11 cells and sometimes more). Although few androecia are present in the specimens I have studied, I could find no evidence of fusion of the bracts (i.e. the saccate portion was not fused with that of the opposite bract). Free androecial bracts are unusual in Chiloscyphus: Engel (2010) noted that only five species have this feature. Specimens of C. howeanus from Lord Howe Island have sometimes been identified as C. bispinosus (Hook.f. & Taylor) J.J.Engel & R.M.Schust., but that species is terrestrial rather than epiphytic, the leaves, gynoecial bracts and androecial bracts are much more deeply divided, gynoecia are infrequent, the intramarginal cells of the leaves are irregularly enlarged, the leaf cells have large and often bulging trigones (at least in robust forms), and the leaves and underleaves are often armed with additional spinose teeth (Engel 2010). A specimen in NSW (NSW-448931) originally identified as C. latifolius is C. howeanus. These two species are easily separated, particularly because C. latifolius is infrequently branched, perianths are rare, and the leaf cells lack trigones (Engel 2010), whereas C. howeanus is commonly branched, perianths are common, and the leaf cells have small but distinct trigones. Overall, Chiloscyphus howeanus seems to connect C. bispinosus, C. excisifolius, C. lentus and C. subporosus.
