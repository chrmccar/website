A suite of morphological characters shared between Frullania congesta and F pty- chantha lend support to their placement in F subg. Diastaloba Spruce. Both species share the following morphological characteristics: 1) the first branch leaf (BLl) with two saccate appendages; 2) rim of perianth beak papillate; 3) comparatively large median cell size; and 4) lobules at angles of (30) 45- 80 (90)° with stem. The spore surface ultrastructure of the two species is also similar. In addition, von Konrat and Braggins (2001a) reported in a sur- vey of the initial branching appendages of Frullania that the presence of two saccate ap- pendages at BL 1 was a consistent feature for many species of subg. Diastaloba. However, Frullania congesta can easily be differentiated from F ptychantha by the following characters: I) large lobules in proportion to the lobes; 2) microphyllous branch- ing absent to inconspicuous; 3) gynoecia frequently producing subfloral innovations and subfloral branches; 4) median cells of the lobe usually with 2-3 oil bodies per cell, and col- lectively the oil bodies frequently occupying up to 112 of the cell lumen; and 5) generally smaller plant size Frullania ptychantha, on the other hand, is characterized by 1) small lob- ules in comparison to the lobe; 2) conspicuous microphyllous branching; 3) gynoecia rarely producing subfloral innovations and subfloral branches; 4) median cells of the lobe usually with 4-6 oil bodies per cell, and collectively the oil bodies only occupying up to 1I6th of the cell lumen; and 5) generally larger plant size. Chemistry Recently, Asakawa et al. (2003) identified and discussed the major chemical compo- nents, detected by TLC and GC/MS analysis, of a selection of Frullania species, including F congesta, F aterrima, F rostrata, and F ptychantha (summarized in Table 2). According to the criteria developed by Asakawa et al. (1981), F congesta, F aterrima, and F rostrata should be assigned to chemo-type 2 (Sesquiterpene lactone-type), and F ptychantha should be assigned to chemo-type 1 (Sesquiterpene lactone-Bibenzyl type). Frullania congesta is rich in sesquiterpene lactones with the major components including dihydrodiplophyllolide (7%), frullanolide, and three unknown sesquiterpene lactones. Frullania aterrima and F rostrata also produce sesquiterpene lactones as the major components, though these are chemically different from F congesta. Frullania rostrata also appears to be chemically more primitive than F congesta and F ptychantha because F rostrata contained two simple germacranolides from which the eudesmane-type sesquiterpene lactones (present in F con- gesta and F ptychantha) are biosynthesized (Asakawa et al. 2003). 560 1. No. 100 200 6 Distribution and Ecology Frullania congesta has a seemingly patchy distribution throughout the New Zealand botanical region (Fig. 4), ranging from sea level to relatively low elevations of c. 450 m. Figure 4 also provides a rudimentary demonstration of the difference in relative abundance between the different areas based on the number of collections in each region. Collecting effort was similar throughout the country. We predict an increase in the number of known present localities, but it is speculated that the pattern of an increasing frequency from northern to southern latitudes will remain an accurate representation of its abundance in nature. Interestingly, the distribution appears to correlate with the amount of rainfall. For instance, in Stewart Island there are over 200 rain days annually (Meteorological Service of New Zealand), which corresponds to the greatest number of collections made throughout New Zealand. It is also apparent that F. congesta is one of only a few Frullania species in New Zealand, which are tolerant to exposure to salt spray. This is significant on two counts: a) liverworts have been known for their extremely low salinity tolerances (Engel & Schus- ter, 1973); and b) Engel & Schuster (1973) reported for Stewart Island a notable lack of liv- erworts in the intertidal zone where sea spray is a factor. Therefore, the amount of precipi- tation coupled with coastal environments, appears to play a significant role for the distribu- tion of this species.
