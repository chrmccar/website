Unfortunately this collection could not be found during this study. However, it is notable that there are no collections by Watts of Heteroscyphus argutus from the island, despite it being very common in Erskine Valley where Watts 76b was collected. Poorly developed specimens of H. argutus are quite common among collections from Lord Howe Island, often bearing only two or three teeth at the apex and with the underleaf armature reduced. It is possible that Watts 76b is merely such a weak form of that species, equivalent to Schiffner’s variety paucidentatus. It is noteworthy that the type, from the Auckland Islands, was growing on the bark of trees (Taylor and Hooker 1845), which is a very unusual habitat for Geocalycaceae generally, and that the species has never been fully described or illustrated, so that the identification of the numerous specimens in New Zealand herbaria (AVH 2018) must be considered tentative. Discussion: Heteroscyphus echinellus was previously known only from New Zealand, Tasmania and Victoria (Engel and He 2010). Lord Howe Island is thus the mostly northerly known point of its distribution. The variety echinellus was thought to have been endemic to New Zealand, with only H. echinellus var. hyalinus J.J.Engel known from Australia (Engel and He 2010). This variety is therefore new to the Australian flora. It is easily distinguished from var. hyalinus even in the field because it has plane rather than downcurved leaf apices, so that the spinose armature around the apex is clearly visible in dorsal view (Engel and He 2010).
