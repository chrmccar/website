Hooker s.n., date unknown, herb. Taylor, not seen. Description: Scott (1985) published a general description of this species that is adequate for identification. Illustrations: Figure 3. Also Scott (1985, Figure 85), Meagher and Fuhrer (2003, pages 264– 265). Habitat: Soil, bark and rotting wood in damp to wet situations. Distribution on LHI: Eastern slopes of Mt Lidgbird, Erskine Valley. Total distribution: Eastern Australia (Tasmania to southern Queensland, Lord Howe I.), New Zealand. Discussion: Heteroscyphus fissistipus is one of the most common species of Heteroscyphus in southern Australia, from Tasmania to southern Queensland. Stephani and Watts (1914) reported Chiloscyphus multifidus Steph. from Lord Howe Island on the basis of a collection by W.W. Watts (presumably Watts LHI 88, NSW-818174). This specimen was not available during the present study. However, C. multifidus appears to be conspecific with H. fissistipus judging by Stephani’s original description (Stephani 1906–1909: 132) and his sketch in the Icones (Stephani 1985) drawn from the type. It is notable that only one other collection identified as C. multifidus exists in Australian and New Zealand herbaria (Clark s.n., MEL-64300A), and is no different from H. fissistipus as circumscribed by Scott (1985). 223 Until Watts LHI 88 can be studied or the status of Heteroscyphus multifidus is clarified, I prefer to treat the species as ‘uncertain’ in the Lord Howe Island flora.
