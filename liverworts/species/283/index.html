Heteroscyphus fissistipus (Hook.f. & Taylor) Schiffn, Oesterr. Bot. Zeit. 60: 172
(1910)

(1845)
Type: NEW ZEALAND: Auckland Islands, J.D. Hooker s.n., date unknown, herb. Taylor, not
seen.
Description: Scott (1985) published a general description of this species that is adequate for
identification.
Illustrations: Figure 3. Also Scott (1985, Figure 85), Meagher and Fuhrer (2003, pages 264–
265).
Habitat: Soil, bark and rotting wood in damp to wet situations.
Distribution on LHI: Eastern slopes of Mt Lidgbird, Erskine Valley.
Total distribution: Eastern Australia (Tasmania to southern Queensland, Lord Howe I.), New
Zealand.
Discussion: Heteroscyphus fissistipus is one of the most common species of Heteroscyphus in
southern Australia, from Tasmania to southern Queensland. Stephani and Watts (1914) reported
Chiloscyphus multifidus Steph. from Lord Howe Island on the basis of a collection by W.W.
Watts (presumably Watts LHI 88, NSW-818174). This specimen was not available during the
present study. However, C. multifidus appears to be conspecific with H. fissistipus judging by
Stephani’s original description (Stephani 1906–1909: 132) and his sketch in the Icones
(Stephani 1985) drawn from the type. It is notable that only one other collection identified as
C. multifidus exists in Australian and New Zealand herbaria (Clark s.n., MEL-64300A), and is
no different from H. fissistipus as circumscribed by Scott (1985).

Balantiopsaceae H.Buch
Balantiopsis Mitt.
Balantiopsis is a largely south temperate genus, although extending into the tropics from
Malesia to Brazil. The genus was monographed by Engel (1968), who recognised 13 species.
Since then another three species have been recognised. Three species are known from Australia.
One species occurs on Lord Howe Island.
