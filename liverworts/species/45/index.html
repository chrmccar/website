Furuki (1991) synonymised Riccardia wattsiana under R. graeffei (Steph.) Hewson, a tropical Asia–Oceania species. However, his description of Riccardia graeffei is at odds with R. wattsiana as described and illustrated by Scott (1985), in particular because the branching in R. graeffei is 1–2-pinnate, but in Scott’s R. wattsiana it is 2–3-pinnate and the ultimate pinnae are of similar length, giving a distinctively palmate appearance to the thalli. Brown (1989) had previously suggested that Scott’s ‘R. wattsiana’ might be the same as R. nitida from New Zealand. I examined the isotype of Riccardia wattsiana (NSW-251628!) and also a high-resolution digital image of the holotype (G-11057!d) and two isotypes (G-12209!d, G-69441!d) of R. graeffei, and conclude that Furuki was correct. What a generation of Australian bryologists have been calling Riccardia wattsiana since the publication of Scott (1985) is therefore another species. I had already identified one specimen from Wilsons Promontory as Riccardia nitida (Colenso) E.A.Hodgs., previously thought to be endemic to New Zealand. After examining a number of other specimens identified as R. wattsiana from southern Australia and tropical Queensland, I believe that Brown (1989) was correct, and Scott’s ‘R. wattsiana’ is indeed R. nitida. Furuki (1991) cited G-12209 as the holotype of Riccardia graeffei, but annotated both that specimen and G-69441 as ‘holotype’ on the packets, probably because both are from the same gathering (Graeffe 1629).
