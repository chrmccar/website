Telaranea centipes Lepidoziaceae                                             TVAN
The delicate, glaucous shoots of this species creep over soil and
other bryophytes in wet habitats, particularly on dry clay banks
in wet scl�rophyll forest. The incubous leaves are 4-fid and
oblong, the sides very much parallel, spreading at a wide angle
from the stem. The cells of the leaves are visible under a hand
lens, aligned in about eight neat rows along the length of each
leaf. The underleaves are deeply 4-fid. Sporophytes arise on basal branches from
narrow, tubular perianths about 5 mm long.
    T dispar (V) is rather dull olive-brown. It is common on soil in wet sclerophyll
forest; and occasional in other damp habitats. The wedge-shaped leaves are 4-fid on
the main stems but only 3-fid on the branches. The underleaves are 3-fid or 4-fid
on the main stems, but mostly 2-fid on the branches. T patentissima (TV) is very
similar to T centipes, but has 3-fid underleaves. T herzogii (TV) is a minute plant
in which the leaves and underleaves are reduced to a pair of long, spine-like lobes.
It is quite common in tree-fern gullies in wet forest and rainforest, creeping
through other bryophytes, but it is probably commonly overlooked because of its
size.

Similar species: Lepidozia glaucophylla, Leptophyllopsis laxa

Chiloecyphus Lophocoleaceae
[most Lophocolea species of Scott ( 1985) belong to this genus]
In the field the differences between this genus and Heteroscyphus are not easily defined.
In Chiloscyphus at least one leaf in each pair is not joined to the corresponding
underleaf, but in Heteroscyphus both leaves are usually joined.

Similar species: *Geocalyx caledonicus, Heteroscyphus spp., Lethocolea pansa, Jungermannia
spp., *Pedinophyllum monoicum, Plagiochila spp.

C. latifoliue Lophocoleaceae                                                    WSTVAN
The shoots of this spec1es (for merly Lophocolea
bidentata) are green to straw-coloured, forming loose wefts
often creeping through other bryophytes in grasslands and
grassy understoreys. The leaves have two long, narrow
spines at the leaf apex, often curved away from each other. The
dorsal margin is more or less straight, but the ventral margin is
distinctly rounded. The underleaves are deeply bifid to
beyond half-way, often with long extra teeth.
