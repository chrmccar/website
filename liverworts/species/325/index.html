Sporophytes arise on basal branches from narrow, tubular perianths about 5 mm long. T dispar (V) is rather dull olive-brown. It is common on soil in wet sclerophyll forest; and occasional in other damp habitats. The wedge-shaped leaves are 4-fid on the main stems but only 3-fid on the branches. The underleaves are 3-fid or 4-fid on the main stems, but mostly 2-fid on the branches. T patentissima (TV) is very similar to T centipes, but has 3-fid underleaves. T herzogii (TV) is a minute plant in which the leaves and underleaves are reduced to a pair of long, spine-like lobes. It is quite common in tree-fern gullies in wet forest and rainforest, creeping through other bryophytes, but it is probably commonly overlooked because of its size.
