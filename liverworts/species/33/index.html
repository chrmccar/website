178 20 Metzgeriales Metzgeriaceae H.Klinggr. The family Metzgeriaceae includes about 250 named species in four genera: Apometzgeria, Austrometzgeria, Metzgeria and Vandiemenia (Tropicos 2016). Only Metzgeria is known from Lord Howe Island. Metzgeria Raddi. So (2002a) noted that Metzgeria includes about 240 named species (So 2002a), of which 58 had been recorded from Australasia and some island groups of the Pacific Ocean. Her treatment reduced those 58 to 18 species, of which seven species have been reported from Australia, nine from New Zealand (Glenny 1998) and three from New Caledonia (So 2002a). However, various authors (e.g. Gradstein and Costa 2003, Costa 2008, Thouvenot et al. 2011) have disagreed with some aspects of So’s treatment; for example, Thouvenot et al. (2011) recognised seven species from New Caledonia. Until a complete world-wide treatment of the genus is undertaken, the taxonomy of Metzgeria will continue to be unsettled. Metzgeria leptoneura and M. decipiens (as M. furcata) have been reported previously from Lord Howe Island (Stephani and Watts 1914, So 2002a). M. lindbergii is new to the island’s flora. Discussion: Grolle (2002) and So (2002a) were I think incorrect in treating Metzgeria decipiens as a synonym of the circumglobal M. furcata. The number of cells covering the midrib on the ventral side distinguishes the two clearly: 2 (rarely 3) in M. decipiens, and 4 (rarely 3) in 179 M. furcata. A further difference is that M. furcata rarely produces gemmae on the margins of unmodified thalli (Atherton et al. 2010), whereas M. decipiens commonly produces such gemmae (Scott 1985). Until a complete assessment of the two taxa can be made, the two should be maintained as separate species. As a result of this confusion the overall distribution of M. decipiens in Australia is unclear. However, from my own observations it is common throughout the eastern seaboard from southern Tasmania to northern New South Wales, but reaching as far north as the Queensland Wet Tropics, where it is uncommon. Metzgeria furcata, the forked veilwort, is a frequent liverwort growing on the bark of a wide range of tree and shrub species and occasionally on rocks. It is a slim, translucent thallose liverwort that forms yellow-green mats of branches about 1mm wide.
