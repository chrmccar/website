Radula aneurysmalis often grows among other byophytes, particu- larly R. £lvi/era, R. tasmanica, Heteroscyphus sp. and Frullania spp. Radula aneurysmalis also grows amongst moss and other hepatics in crevices in rock at high altitudes. Recognition: Radula aneurysmalis is distinct in its extremely small size, with shoots less than 350 pm wide, and in its subequal lobes and lobules. Radula aneurysmalis is superficially similar to smaller members of the Lejeuneaceae, such as Drepanolejeunea and Harpalejeunea , however is immediately identified by its I) lack of underleaves, 2) production of rhizoids on the ventral surface of the lobule, never on the stem, 3) the single large oil body per lobe cell, and 4) the dorso-ventrally flattened peri- anths, that are never inflated. Radula aneurysmalis can be confused with R. physoloba, as extensive growth of mi- crophyllous branches can occur in R. physoloba, particularly when plants of the latter grow above tree line. However, these branches can always be seen to originate from the large full sized axes they grow with, and they never produce antheridia or gynoecia. Production of reproductive organs from microphyllous branches is indicative of R. aneurysmalis. Radula aneurysmalis can also be distinguished from microphyllous axes of R. physoloba by a number of features which are summarised in Table I. Radula aneUly smalis bears a particular resemblance to R. compacta, best expressed in the form of the lobules preceding the gynoecia. These two species are separable only by the persistent microphyllous habit of R. aneurysmalis, and the association of microphyllous axes with axes of normal stature in R. compacta.
