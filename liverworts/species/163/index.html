    In the field this species resembles a miniature cross between
    Marsupidium and Tylimanthus, which are close relatives of
    Acrobolbus. It is confined to wet forest and rainforest, especially )



D
    Nothofagus rainforest, usually creeping over or among other
    bryophytes on the trunks of trees. The shoots are minute, usually less
    than 1 mm wide, with a rather grey-green colour caused by a combination of a papillose
    surface and brown oil bodies in the cells. The succubous leaves are unequally bilobed,
    like Tylimanthus tenellus. The smaller lobe can be quite prominent or almost absent.
    Although locally common in suitable habitats, in overall terms this species is rare in
    the region.

    Similar species: Marsupidium spp., Tylimanthus spp.

The characteristic marsupium of this species is 3-4 mm long and
hairy, and arises from a short branch at the base of the stem. However,
the marsupium tends to break away if the plant is pulled from the
substratum, making identification harder. The dark green shoots are
10-30 mm tall, usually erect and often mixed with other bryophytes
on logs and tree trunks in Nothofagus forest. The stems are almost bare of leaves at
the 'base, becoming leafier towards the apex but with the largest leaves often in mid­
stem. The concave leaves are almost circular (sometimes longer and flat in young
shoot�1, succubously inserted but often seemingly transverse. The leaf margins are
almost entire, usually having the hint of one and sometimes two teeth at the apex,
occasionally with a small sinus between them. In Acrobolbus and Tylimanthus the

[Herzogobryum erosum of Scott (1985)]
This is an uncommon plant of rock outcrops and roadside cuttings in
alpine regions. Outside Tasmania it is known only from the Bogong
High Plains and Mt Kosciuszko. The narrow shoots are prostrate but
packed together to form green to brownish pads on soil in rock crevices

in alpine areas. The leaves are very shortly bifid, more or less transverse
but tightly appressed to the stem, so that the stem is invisible from any angle.
Microscopically the cells of the leaf margin have thickened walls that project beyond
the margin. We have never ·seen capsules, although they are reportedly common.
     Herzogobryum teres (TV) is almost indistinguishable in the field. Microscopically
the cells of the_leaf margin are thin-walled. It too is an entirely alpine species.
     Gymnomitrion incompletum (TVN) is a common species that grows in similar
habitats. The shoots are upright and rather greyish green, and the leaves are bifid to
about 1/3. It is unusual in that the capsules do not form in a perianth, but are instead
surrounded by enlarged leaves.
